Flood routing · #chattanooga

A message on #chattanooga, scoped us-tn-cha

Flood routing means every repeater that hears the packet and holds a matching region rebroadcasts it, so it spreads outward in waves rather than following one path. Most Chattanooga repeaters carry us-tn-cha, so the message covers the metro in two or three hops. The occasional repeater that was never given any regions at all simply doesn't carry it — and everyone behind it goes quiet.

Most of the city carries it

Every repeater holding us-tn-cha rebroadcasts, so a message sent downtown reaches the far edge of the metro without anyone routing it by hand.

An unconfigured repeater cuts people off

One repeater on this hop has no regions set at all, so nothing ever matches and it drops everything it hears. The companion behind it — correctly configured, sitting on us-tn-cha — never gets the message, through no fault of its own.

Why this matters for coverage

A repeater with no regions set isn't just failing itself — it silently cuts off everyone downstream of it. The companion on the other side did everything right and still hears nothing. If you're standing up a repeater inside the metro, setting us-tn-cha is what keeps your neighbors reachable.

The cost of two states on one repeater

A repeater that carries both states becomes a door between them. Filtering happens after the packet is received, not before — so every neighbor in earshot demodulates the whole thing before deciding it isn't theirs. Region scoping saved the second hop across the line. It never saved the first.

Region Scoping ✓ The Right Way

The Right Way

The same overlap trick from The Wrong Way — done at the right scale, with the right scopes.

Not for critical infrastructure

Skip this on any repeater with 25+ neighbors. Every extra scope it holds means every one of those neighbors demodulates and discards a packet that was never meant for them — and that cost multiplies with every neighbor listening.

Right for neighborhood repeaters

A small repeater with just a handful of neighbors can hold a second, narrow local scope to fill a dead zone. The waste stays proportional to how few people are actually in earshot.

Ask before you configure

If you live right along the border and are putting one of these on the roof of your home, this will likely cause no issues at all. Still — consult your local mesh first, whether that's over MeshCore channels, Discord, Signal, or however your community coordinates. Don't be afraid to ask people for help. We all love radio, and we're all excited to help new people get on the mesh.

Matches at the border, on purpose

The state line repeater holds us-tn-cha specifically so it can bridge this exact local traffic — it's the whole reason it carries an extra scope.

Small, acceptable waste

Dalton R1 receives it, finds no match, and discards it. On a repeater with only a couple of neighbors, that's a fair price for closing a dead zone.

Correctly contained

The state line repeater doesn't hold us-tn, so it receives the packet, finds no match, and stops. One hop of unavoidable airtime at the border — and nothing at all past it. Dalton's repeaters never hear it.

The Right Way, summarized

Bridge only the narrow, local-only scopes, and only on small repeaters with a handful of neighbors. Keep the state-wide scopes off the border entirely — then they'll never cost you a single hop beyond it.
